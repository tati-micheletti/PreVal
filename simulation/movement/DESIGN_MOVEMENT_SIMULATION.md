# Smurfs: a movement simulation for the PreVal engine (caribou-type), the twin of the Tweety (birds-type) simulation

Written 2026-11-05. Status: DESIGN, GENERATOR PROTOTYPE, UNIT TESTS, ONE LOCAL SMOKE RUN OF THE ENGINE. No simulated world has been trained for a result, so every prediction in section 9 is written before any result exists.
Names (Tati, 2026-11-05): **Smurfs** = this movement simulation, **Tweety** = the birds-type simulation (`simulation/ANALYSIS_PLAN_SIMULATION.md`, `simulation/simulate_world.R`). File names keep the old words (`simulate_movement.R`).
Files (all new, all in `simulation/movement/`): `simulate_movement.R` (generator), `test_simulate_movement.R` (46 checks), `diagnose_movement.R` (realism-anchor statistics; also runs on the real table), `diagnose_default_world_output.txt` (its output on the default world), `smoke_engine_movement.R` (engine smoke test), this document.
Nothing outside `simulation/movement/` was edited.

## 1. Why a second simulation
The Tweety answer "does flipping explain PreVal's advantage?" for count data. Caribou is the second data type of the paper, and there the flipping group is the movement block (step length, turn angle and the nine step-length interactions), which follows the collar fix schedule (`caribou/transferability_screen_reading.md`; the yearly `logSl` effect tracks the mean observed log step length, Spearman 0.99). The Smurfs let us set (a) how many covariates flip, (b) whether they are habitat effects or schedule effects, (c) how much animal-specific memory exists and whether it is stable or drifts, with the true coefficients known. Same hypotheses as the Tweety, same engine, other data type. It is a separate, labelled controlled experiment and is never mixed with real data.

## 2. What is reused from the old smurf scripts
The old scripts (`Simulated Data/`: `move_smurf.R`, `move_gargamel.R`, `move_villain.R`, `move_villan_smurfs.R`, `Simulated Data 2.R`) are a rule-based agent model: smurfs flee Gargamel and Azrael, go to the village, stay together, look for mushrooms. They produce tracks, not used-versus-available steps, and have no known selection coefficients. Reused ideas only:

| Old element | Role now |
|---|---|
| village attraction (`villageAttaction`) | `homePull`: a weak pull of every animal towards its home centre (range fidelity); omitted from the candidate columns, as in the real data |
| mushroom attraction / terrain gradient | habitat layers: smooth landscape fields the animal selects (stable, drifting, flipping, noise) |
| group cohesion | herds: animals of a herd share a home area and part of their individual preferences |
| `max_smurf_step`, step budget | the step-length kernel (gamma) |
| villains, threat distance, repulsion | not used as agents; avoidance of a layer is a negative habitat coefficient |
| Gargamel / Azrael pursuit, capture, cool-down, terrain factor | not used (game-specific) |

## 3. The world
- **Animals and years.** 2013-2022, the real number of animals per year (26, 26, 34, 33, 36, 48, 55, 64, 61, 58) and the real number of new animals per year (26, 6, 10, 3, 14, 16, 8, 19, 10, 14; 126 animals; 76% of a year's animals were also present the year before, as in the real table). About 663 steps per animal-year (real mean; lognormal spread assumed), giving about 300,000 strata in the default world (real: 292,579).
- **Space.** A 400 x 400 km box in projected metres. Six herds with home centres; each animal's home is its herd centre plus 25 km scatter. Nine habitat layers are smooth random fields with patches of about 1.5 km and a regional scale of about 40 km, evaluated at any point (no raster).
- **A step.** From its position the animal looks at 11 candidate steps drawn from a FIXED availability kernel (gamma step length with median about 390 m, von Mises turn angle with kappa 0.3: "the kernel fitted on the pooled data") and chooses one with probability proportional to exp(eta). The chosen one is the observed step, the other 10 are the available steps. This is exactly the conditional logit the engine and the real analysis assume (`nCandidates = 11`, default), so a conditional logit on the identifiable columns estimates the true coefficients (tested, section 11). With `nCandidates > 11` the observed step is chosen among more candidates and the 10 available steps are fresh kernel draws (closer to the continuous step-selection model, slower, coefficients slightly attenuated; checked for format only).
- **Chance.** The loss of a model that knows nothing is log 11 = 2.398 per stratum, as in the real data.

## 4. The selection function with known truth
eta (of a candidate step) = habitat + movement + animal. The within-stratum variance of eta is split by targets (shares), then a world-wide `etaSd` (0.6) sets the signal size. Default shares: stable 0.40, drifting 0.05, flipping habitat 0.10, movement 0.15, animal 0.30.

**Habitat (value of each layer at the END of the step).** Families as in the Tweety: **stable** (constant coefficient), **drift** (same sign, strength changing linearly over the years, `driftRate`), **flip** (coefficient `c_j (sqrt(1 - phi^2) + phi s_j(t))` with `s_j(t)` a +-1 sign: `flipMode = "random"`, each flipper with its own chain changing with probability `flipProb` per year, or `"regime"`, ONE chain shared by all flippers with mean length `regimeLength` years; `phi = flipStrength`, 0 = stable = the matched control), **noise** (no effect). The default 9 layers follow the real screen: 4 stable (wetland, mixed forest, vegetation, distance to paved roads), 2 drift (the two disturbance clocks, same sign, changing size), 1 flip (needleleaf cover), 2 noise.

**Movement block (the flipping group of the real data).** Coefficients of `logSl`, `cosTa` and the nine interactions `logSl x habitat at the START of the step`. They are the tilt of the true movement kernel against the fixed availability kernel, and ONE shared year pattern drives all of them (one flipping group, not ten, as the real screen concluded). `moveMode`: `"schedule"` (default: the year pattern is the standardised real mean observed log step length by year, so the world has the real collar-schedule pattern), `"random"`, `"regime"` (sign chains, as for habitat flips), `"none"` (stable block). `moveFlipStrength` plays the role of phi. Observable consequence (tested): the yearly mean of observed minus available log step length follows the effective `logSl` coefficient. In the default world the yearly mean observed log step length has an sd of 0.21 (real 0.18).

**Which columns exist.** For each of 9 layers `<layer>_start` (constant within a stratum, no effect, cancels in the conditional logit), `<layer>_end`, `inter_logSl_x_<layer>_start`; plus `logSl`, `cosTa`, `sinTa` (no effect). 30 candidates, 21 identifiable, as in the real table. Names are neutral and shuffled (`mv_cov_001` ...). The coefficient of a column is the coefficient on the column AS SUPPLIED, so `trueBeta` (column x year) is the exact truth, not an approximation.

**Truth kept.** `trueBeta` per column and year, `flipSigns` (habitat flippers), `moveSigns`/`moveShape` (movement pattern), `covariates` (role, layer, family, block, within-stratum sd, peak effect = max over years of |beta| x sd), the animal table (home, herd), the individual preferences per animal, layer and year (`deviations`), `scales`. Functions: `movementContributions`, `oracleLoss` (cross-entropy of the true model; the default world gains 0.118 below chance, 0.078 without the animal term and the home pull), `worldDiagnostics`, `memoryValue` (section 6).

## 5. Animal effect, year fingerprint, trend
- **Animal (individual) effect.** An animal's overall level cancels inside a stratum, so the only thing an animal can carry is a different *preference*. Each animal has its own deviations of the habitat coefficients (random slopes on the effect layers), partly shared by the animals of a herd (60% spatial field with 60 km range, 40% independent). They carry the `animal` share of the variance (default 0.30). This is what the 8-d animal embedding of the network can learn (the Tweety route effect).
- **Year fingerprint (`yearShare` 0.25).** Layers carry a year-wide shift. It is constant inside a stratum, so it cancels in the conditional logit, but the network sees absolute values and can recognise a year; with random validation it can then learn year-specific signs. With 0 the signs cannot be tied to a year through the covariates.
- **Trend (`trend`).** Shift of the dynamic layers over the series, in SD units (novelty of the later years).
- **Home pull.** `homePull` 1 gives a median distance from the home centre of 11 km (home range parameter 15 km); an omitted extra term (sd 0.04 of eta) that makes tracks locally dependent, as range fidelity does in nature.
- **Common random numbers.** Landscape, animals, herds, homes, composition, individual preferences and the innovations of every step are seeded by components and drawn in lock-step by (year, step, animal), so worlds that differ in one coefficient parameter share all of them (tested). Paths separate after the first different choice: the matched control is exact for the world components, statistical for the tracks.

## 6. The memory factor (added 2026-11-05, Tati: for the Science-paper evidence list)
**Question.** Does memory help at short horizons and go stale at long ones? In a conditional-logit design memory is the animal-specific preference and nothing else. The engine's animal embedding is the only place where it can live.

**Generator mechanism: drifting individual preferences.** The deviation of animal i on layer l in year t is `tau (sqrt(1 - d) u_il + sqrt(d) w_il(t))`: a fixed part `u` and a part `w` that drifts as an AR(1) over the years with persistence `animalDriftRho` (0.6 default). `animalDriftShare = d` (default 0.5) is the share of the individual variance that drifts; 0 = fully fixed, 1 = fully drifting. The variance is the same in every year, so the animal share of eta does not depend on d. The correlation of an animal's preferences between years s and t is `(1 - d) + d rho^|t - s|` (tested).

**Known truth for memory.** `memoryValue(w, lags)` gives the cross-entropy of the true model with (a) no animal term and (b) the animal's preferences of `lag` years earlier. The difference is the most that a memory learned `lag` years ago can be worth at horizon `lag`. It is constant in lag for fixed preferences and falls towards 0 (below 0 for independent years) for drifting ones; the test checks this. This truth is the yardstick for what the networks should show.

**Engine side: embedding on / off.** On = the engine as it is (8-d embedding concatenated to the step covariates; unseen animals use the mean embedding). Off = `embDim = 0` through the module parameter `hyperOverride` ("route information off"). `caribou/runMe_caribou.R` does NOT pass that parameter on, so the factor cannot be switched with the existing script. It was tested with a COPY of the script with one added argument (`hyperOverride = envChr("PREVAL_HYPER", "")`; `smoke_engine_movement.R ... memoryOff` writes the copy next to its outputs; the original is untouched). The run trained 18 models with `embDim 0` (confirmed in the settings stamp) and analysed them. A permanent switch in `runMe_caribou.R` is a one-line change for Tati or the EVE agent. Because `embDim` is part of the settings stamp, memory-off runs need their own tag, as in the copy.

**Cells (stage M, section 8).** Embedding {on, off} x drift share {0, 0.5, 1} x horizon {1, 4, 7} (see question 5 for the choice of horizons); PreVal and status quo; natural ladder. In the default world (drift share 0.5, rho 0.6) the true value of memory falls from 0.040 (current preferences) to 0.026, 0.018, 0.014 and 0.010 at lags 1-4 and stays near 0.010 at lags 5-6 (the fixed half keeps its value); with drift share 0 it is constant, with share 1 and rho 0 it is negative beyond lag 0 (stale preferences hurt).

## 7. How a caribou-type table is produced for the engine
`writeMovementWorld(w, dir)` writes `final_caribou.rds` (the long table: 11 rows per stratum, the observed step first; `indiv_step_id`, `id`, `year`, `x1_`, `y1_`, `case_`, `burst_`, then the 30 candidate columns), `truth_caribou.rds`, `featureTable.csv` (the 30 candidates, Importance NA) and `networkSettings.rds` (the original caribou network: 8-d embedding, 128-64, learning rate 0.001, batch 128, as `caribouNetworkSettings()` writes it). The engine reads exactly these three files (`caribou/runMe_caribou.R`: `PREVAL_FINAL` = the folder, `PREVAL_OUT` = its parent, folder name `caribou`), plus its own design files. The prep stage is not run (it reads the real prepared table, which is not used here). Neither the table nor the files need an adapter. `burst_` is the animal-year index (one burst per animal-year; the real first-step drop is not mimicked). The engine's own `buildStrataCaseIndex()` and the prep module's `checkCaribouFinal()` accept the table (tested). The years 2013-2022 match the engine defaults (`PREVAL_START` 2013, `PREVAL_END` 2022).

## 8. Ladder orders and factorial cells
**Ladder orders** (same four as the Tweety). `natural` is the engine's own per-window ranking (nothing to supply). The three manipulated orders exist only in the simulation: `ladderRanking(w, "flippersFirst" | "flippersLast" | "random")` and `oracleRanking(w)` return tables in the engine's per-window ranking format (`run<tag>/rankings/s<start>_e<end>.csv`). "Flipping columns" = the habitat flip layers' end columns and, when `moveMode` is not "none", the whole movement block (family labels are kept in the matched control phi = 0, as in the Tweety). Of the 30 columns 16 carry an effect (7 end, 7 interaction, `logSl`, `cosTa`); 14 carry none (9 `_start`, the noise layers' columns, `sinTa`). At levels 2, 5 and 10 the orders differ; at 20 only in which null columns enter; at 30 (all) they coincide.

**Stage 0, realism anchor (before any flip world is read).** The default world (movement flips with the real schedule, one weak habitat flipper), 3 real-size worlds plus 3 reduced worlds (250 steps per animal-year, about 0.4 of the strata, to check that the pattern survives the size cut), arms `main` (PreVal, status quo, Internal), `newAnimals` (PreVal, status quo, animal-grouped CV), `newPlaces` (PreVal, status quo, spatial CV, PreVal + spatial), natural ladder, levels 2, 5, 10, 20, 30, horizons 1 and 4.

**Stage 1a, same animals, PreVal against status quo** (`main` arm; levels 2, 5, 10 for each of the 4 orders and 30 once). Factors: F1 flip strength phi {0, 1} (0.85 moves to stage 2 to save compute), F2 flip share {0.10, 0.30}, F3 ladder order (4), **F5 where the flips sit {habitat layers only (movement block stable), movement block only (habitat flippers off)}**. F5 is new against the Tweety: habitat flips are the clean analogue of the Tweety flips (ecology), movement flips are the real caribou situation (collar schedule). 2 x 2 x 2 = 8 world configurations x 3 replicate worlds (the unit of inference is the forecast year, 8 years 2015-2022; replicates are averaged inside a year) = 24 worlds. Habitat-flip worlds use 3 flip layers (3 stable, 1 drift, 3 flip, 2 noise). Primary quantity as in the Tweety: `DiD = (PreVal - SQ in the flip world) - (PreVal - SQ in the matched phi = 0 world)` per forecast year.

**Stage M, memory.** Default world with animal share 0.30 and 0.45, drift share {0, 0.5, 1}, embedding {on, off}, horizons {1, 4, 7}, PreVal and status quo, natural ladder, levels 2, 5, 10, 30; 3 reduced replicate worlds per cell.

**Stage 2 (one factor at a time from the reference, phi 1, share 0.30, movement flips, natural and flippers-first ladders, 2 replicates, reduced worlds):** animal share 0.05 / 0.30 / 0.45; year fingerprint 0 / 0.25; trend 0 / 1.5; habitat flipProb 0.1 / 0.3 / 0.5; flip rule random / regime (length 3, 6); movement pattern schedule / random / regime; steps per animal-year 160 / 663 (serial dependence of the tracks); `etaSd` 0.4 / 0.6 / 0.8; home pull 0 / 1 / 4; `nCandidates` 11 / 40 (checks that the choice-set shortcut does not matter). About 15 cells.

**Stage 3 (decision table):** the reference and four extreme cells, all three arms, natural and flippers-first ladders, 3 reduced replicates.

Known deviations of the default world, to be fixed in stage 0 before any flip world is read: (1) the movement block carries 0.12-0.15 of the variance, but the real movement terms carry most of the importance; the share is set by the yearly swing of the mean log step length, which an interaction-heavy block inflates (movement and interaction coefficients share one year pattern). (2) Realised within-stratum sd of eta is about 15% above the target (0.69 vs 0.60): the shares are calibrated on pilot positions before the animals select habitat, and selecting animals end up where the habitat varies more. Realised shares scatter by up to 0.12 around the targets (stable high, movement low); `worldDiagnostics` reports the realised ones. A second calibration pass would remove it (not done). (3) Gaussian layers, no distance-type (skewed) layers, one burst per animal-year, no first-step drop, collar schedule only as a yearly pattern (not a within-year mixture of 4 h and 8 h fixes).
**Real-table statistics are one command away:** `Rscript simulation/movement/diagnose_movement.R --table <path to the real final_caribou.rds>` prints the same statistics (structure, steps by year, block occupancy, yearly sign changes, a pooled conditional-logit forecast) for the real table, so the anchor can be read side by side. I did not run it (the real data are not used in this task).
**Proposed tolerances (to be fixed by Tati before any simulated model is trained):** status quo excess over chance within +0.01 to +0.06; PreVal within +-0.015 of chance; PreVal - status quo between -0.06 and -0.015; H1 between +0.015 and +0.045, positive in at least 7 of 8 years; |PreVal - spatial CV| < 0.01 in new places; |PreVal - animal CV| < 0.01. Table-level targets: strata per animal-year +-10%, sd of yearly mean log step length within 0.15-0.25, flipPrimary count 8-12 of 21. If the default world cannot reproduce the regime pattern, the generator is changed (movement share, animal share, `etaSd`, `yearShare`, `homePull`, drift share) and the change is logged BEFORE any flip world is read.

**Predictions (forecast year = unit, 8 years, smallest exact two-sided p = 0.008, so only 8/8 or 7/8 can be significant; negative = first regime better; equivalence margin 0.005).**
- **M1 (anchor):** the default world reproduces the tolerances above. A failure is a generator problem, not a result.
- **M2 (leak alone; DIFFERS from the Tweety S1):** at phi = 0 and moveFlipStrength = 0, PreVal - status quo is already negative, about -0.005 to -0.03, because consecutive steps share places and the status quo's random validation sits next to its training strata (the real H1 of +0.03 says the same). I do not predict |PreVal - SQ| < 0.02 as in the Tweety. This is why the DiD, not the raw contrast, is primary.
- **M3 (flipping works if flippers are in the ladder; Tweety S2):** phi >= 0.85 (1 in stage 1a), flip share 0.30, flippers-first: DiD <= -0.015 at levels >= 5, negative in at least 7 of 8 years.
- **M4 (the engine's ranking; Tweety S3):** with habitat flippers the natural-ladder DiD is smaller than the flippers-first one at levels <= 10 (the engine demotes sign-changing covariates). With movement flippers natural and flippers-first agree within 0.01, because the movement terms stay near the top of the natural ladder (real: 7 of the top 10 are movement terms).
- **M5 (dose-response; S4):** |DiD| grows with phi and with flip share.
- **M6 (mechanism; S5):** with `yearShare` 0 the habitat-flip DiD disappears (|DiD| < 0.01). For movement flips it shrinks by at least half but may not vanish, because animal cohorts also identify years.
- **M7 (animal memory; S6):** a larger animal share makes PreVal worse relative to the status quo at horizon 1 (PreVal's validation animals are removed from training): the difference rises by at least 0.01 between animal share 0.05 and 0.45.
- **M8 (novelty; S7):** with trend 1.5 the DiD is larger in later forecast years, and the slope of PreVal - SQ on log(DI) is negative for phi > 0 and about 0 for phi = 0.
- **M9 (order; S9):** with phi >= 0.85 and share 0.30 the DiD at levels 5 and 10 is most negative with flippers first, near 0 with flippers last, natural and random in between; at "all" the four orders agree within 0.01.
- **M10 (regime vs random flips; S10):** same sign, size may differ.
- **M11 (where the flips sit; new):** habitat flips and movement flips of equal share both give a negative DiD, ratio between 0.5 and 2. If the movement DiD is about 0 while the habitat DiD is negative, schedule flips do not help PreVal and the real-data gain has another source (serial dependence, animal memory).
- **M12 (serial dependence; new):** with fewer steps per animal-year (160) the status quo's optimism (reported minus realised loss) is smaller and H1 smaller than at 663.
- **MEM1 (stable memory helps):** drift share 0, embedding on beats embedding off by at least 0.01 for the status quo and for PreVal at horizon 1, and keeps at least 70% of that benefit at horizon 7.
- **MEM2 (drifting memory goes stale):** drift share 1 (rho 0.6): the embedding benefit at horizon 1 is at least 0.005, at horizon 4 less than half of it, at horizon 7 at most 0.002 (no benefit; a harm of up to +0.005 is allowed).
- **MEM3 (the slope):** the benefit of the embedding falls with horizon, with a slope that is about 0 for drift share 0 and negative for 0.5 and 1; the sizes follow `memoryValue` in order but are smaller (a network cannot learn the true preferences exactly).
- **MEM4:** with the embedding off the status quo's optimism is smaller than with it on (memory is one route of the leak), but PreVal - SQ stays negative (the leak through neighbouring steps is not memory).
- **MEM5 (PreVal and memory):** PreVal's unseen-animal validation ties the embedding to animals seen in training; PreVal with the embedding off loses less than the status quo with it off.

**Support / against.** Same reading as the Tweety: flipping causes the advantage if M3, M5, M6 hold together; leak or capacity alone if M2 is large and the DiD about 0; mixed if both; neither if all contrasts are within the margin. Any outcome is reported as found.
