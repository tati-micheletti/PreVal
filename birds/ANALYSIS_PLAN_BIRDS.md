# Birds: analysis plan (written 2026-10-17, BEFORE any model is run)

Amendments are dated and labelled. Anything decided after seeing results is exploratory and says so.

## Scope
- **Two species, each its own experiment:** greenfinch (*Chloris chloris*, EURING 16490) and great spotted woodpecker (*Dendrocopos major*, 8760). Species are NOT a variable: each has its own covariate ranking, its own ladder, its own network settings and its own results. Species are only compared in the summary.
- **Response:** `Reviere` (territory count per route and year), 2005-2024, negative-binomial loss. Rows with `Reviere` = -1 are excluded (observer could not give an exact count; no territory count exists).
- **Unit:** route (fixed location, observed in many years). **Final table for modelling:** one row per route and year with the response, route id, coordinates, spatial block, and all covariates.
- **Covariates (no pruning of correlated ones in the main analysis, as in birdMonitor; ranking by permutation importance with highly correlated covariates, |r| > 0.9, permuted together; the effective number of covariates, from a principal-component count at 90% variance, is reported next to the nominal count):** the ones already prepared (1 km land use and cover, elevation, slope, solar radiation, distance to woodland; climate from CHELSA and the 50 km bioclim windows; 200 m summaries inside each route) plus the extra ones from the predictor review (forest and human layers). Nothing already prepared is dropped. Landscape heterogeneity is rebuilt for 2005-2020 from the land-use and land-cover layers. The beech/oak mast index is NOT used (not needed for a regime comparison).

## Design (same as the caribou refit)
- Split = history window s..e and forecast year T > e. Test set = a fixed random half of the routes of year T, identical in all regimes.
- **PreVal:** train s..e-1, validate on year e. **Status quo:** the same route-years re-allocated at random to train and validation. **Route-grouped CV (added 2026-10-17):** same pool, validation = whole routes (all their years) chosen at random, no buffer; it separates route-identity leakage from spatial autocorrelation. **Spatial-block CV:** same pool, validation = whole spatial blocks of routes (blockCV; block size from the residual-autocorrelation range of a baseline model), training excludes the validation blocks and a buffer. **Random CV reference:** random draw from s..T minus the test set (leaky; used only as the H1 reference, never compared with PreVal).
- **Balance (amended 2026-10-17):** equal pool, equal validation size (PreVal's), equal test set, shared model seeds per cell, and EQUAL TRAINING SIZE: every regime is randomly subsampled to the training size of the spatial-block regime (which loses its buffer). Splits where spatial blocking leaves less than 80% of the status-quo training set are skipped. Leak gates stop the run on any violation; a hard unit test checks zero overlap.
- **Network:** one scoring network per species, settings chosen by a pilot on pre-test history only (never on test years), then FIXED and identical for every regime and every ladder level of that species. Output head: negative binomial.
- **Ladder:** covariates ranked by permutation importance of a global model per species; levels 2, 5, 10, 15, 25, 35, 50 and beyond if enough covariates exist.
- **Inference:** the forecast year is the independent unit; exact sign-flip tests across years, t intervals, plus a mixed model on all splits. Shape of the complexity curve: PEAK and END contrasts and the mixed-model regime x count interaction (as in caribou amendment 4), and the per-year stability of each covariate's coefficient (negative-binomial model per year, heterogeneity I-squared and sign flips) as a pre-declared diagnostic.

## Predictions (Tati, 2026-10-17, and the engine's checks)
1. **H1:** the status quo's forecast loss is larger than the random-CV reference's, and the status quo's reported error understates its forecast error (optimism > 0).
2. **H2:** PreVal forecasts better than the status quo.
3. **Spatial-block CV** is not as good as PreVal but better than the status quo: forecast loss PreVal < spatial-block < status quo. Reason for the second half: in a panel the same route appears in many years, so random validation lets a route's other years sit in training; blocks remove that route-level leakage but still validate inside the training years.
4. **Route-grouped CV** (our addition): forecast loss status quo >= route-grouped >= spatial-block >= PreVal (weakly ordered). The gap from the status quo to route-grouped measures route-identity leakage, route-grouped to spatial-block the spatial neighbours, spatial-block to PreVal the forward step in time.
4b. **Optimism** (forecast loss minus the loss the validation reported): largest for the status quo, intermediate for spatial-block CV, smallest for PreVal.
5. **Covariate ladder:** similar to caribou. Under PreVal the loss does not rise with the number of covariates (flat or slightly improving). Under the status quo the loss rises and may form a hump at intermediate counts, driven by covariates whose relationship with the counts changes over time.
6. **Risk noted in advance:** because routes repeat across years, random CV may leak more than in caribou, so the status-quo optimism may be larger than in caribou.

## Sensitivity analyses
- S1: the same experiment with 2 km spatial thinning of the route-years.
- S2: block size x0.5 and x2.
- Optional, only if a reviewer asks (decided 2026-10-17 not to plan it): pruning of correlated covariates (|r| < 0.7, birdMonitor's threshold). The effective number of covariates is reported instead.
- S3 (potential improvement, later): other algorithms on the same splits (birdMonitor's `nnet`, BRT) to see whether the conclusions depend on the network type.

## Rules
Predictions are not changed after seeing results. Differences between this plan and what is run are listed in the results document.

## Amendments of 2026-10-17 (made while building the code, BEFORE any real run; no model result has been looked at)
1. **Network settings by pilot.** For each species a pilot fits a small grid (hidden layers 32-16, 64-32, 128-64, 128-64-32; route embedding 0 or 8; weight decay 0 or 1e-4; dropout 0 or 0.1) on the PRE-TEST history only: train on the earlier years, validate on the last pre-test year (PreVal logic). The smallest network within 0.002 of the best validation loss is kept and then FIXED for every regime, ladder level and forecast year of that species. Pre-test history = years up to the year before the first forecast year of the design.
2. **Covariate ladder.** Primary ranking: permutation importance of one global model with a random 20% hold-out over all years (what a practitioner with the usual random validation would obtain; same as the caribou refit). Sensitivity ladder: the same procedure with a FORWARD hold-out on strictly pre-test years only (the ordering PreVal's own logic gives, never using a forecast year). Correlated covariates (|r| > 0.9) are permuted together and share the group's importance; within a group they are ordered by marginal association with the response. The ordering is identical in all regimes, so it cannot favour a regime by construction.
3. **Design parameters.** Analysis years 2005-2024; history window s..e of at least 5 years; forecast distance 1 year (T = e + 1); every start year s that gives such a window. Spatial blocks: block size = the range of the residual spatial autocorrelation (blockCV::cv_spatial_autocor) of a negative-binomial baseline (top-10 ranked covariates + year factor) fitted to the pre-test rows, route-averaged residuals; buffer = a quarter of the block size; whole blocks (random order) are held out until the PreVal validation size is reached. Splits where blocking leaves less than 80% of the status-quo training pool are skipped and listed (splitsSkipped.csv); they are mostly early, short windows where the PreVal validation year is a large share of the pool.
4. **Land-use hedges.** The land-use product has a hedges class only in 2017-2021 and 2024; in those years hedges are carved out of grassland, orchards and fallow, which would act as a structural break (a covariate whose meaning changes in time). Main analysis: one merged class `green_permanent` = grassland + orchards_and_berries + fallow + hedges (hedges only in the years where they are real) plus a static hedge density per route. Sensitivity (S5): the classes as delivered.
5. **Response.** `Reviere` = -1 (observer could not give an exact number) rows are excluded (4 greenfinch and 5 woodpecker route-years). Primary scores: negative-binomial negative log-likelihood per case (reported on the regime's own validation set, realised on the shared test set); the forecast year is the unit of inference.
6. **Not changed:** all predictions above, the five regimes, equal training size, the pre-specified contrasts (H1 = status quo minus Internal; PreVal is never compared with Internal).

## Amendments of 2026-10-18 (decisions of Tati; still before any real run; they supersede the design parameters of the 2026-10-17 amendments)
1. **Primary ladder = random-holdout permutation importance over all years** (as in caribou). Rationale: every regime gets the same ordering, so the ordering cannot favour a regime; the aim is a regime comparison, not the best model. The forward ordering (strictly pre-test years) stays as a sensitivity ladder.
2. **Design.** Analysis years 2005-2024; PreVal windows with at least 2 TRAINING years; forecast distances 1 to 5 years (as in caribou, where the PreVal advantage grew with the horizon: -0.010 at 1 year, about -0.05 at 3 or more); spatial buffer = block size / 8 (4.5 km for a 36 km block). With these settings the first forecast year that survives the 80% training rule is 2009 (greenfinch 593 splits, 23,720 models; woodpecker 592 splits, 23,680 models), so the network pilot uses years <= 2008 and the design refuses any forecast year <= the last pilot year.
3. **Small Woody Features (Copernicus) are NOT used**: the Thuenen hedge layer is already in (static hedge density per route).
4. **Route information in the network (fixed by design, not chosen by the pilot).** Main analysis: a route INTERCEPT, one learned number per route added to the predicted log-mean and shrunk by an L2 penalty (the network analogue of the random route effect of mixed models and of the route effects of the BBS hierarchical models); the penalty strength is tuned in the pilot on pre-test years. Sensitivity: no route information; and an 8-dimensional route embedding fed with the covariates (as in the caribou network). Units unseen in training use the mean of the trained rows. Prediction: the gain from route information is larger where routes are shared between training and validation (status quo) than where they are not (route-grouped, spatial, PreVal), i.e. it is a leakage channel; we report the status-quo optimism under all three settings.
5. **Two-year validation arm (extra arm, Tati's hypothesis).** PreVal validates on the last TWO years of the window (training >= 2 years before them); the other regimes keep the same validation size (twice as large), the same equal training size and the same shared test set. Run as a separate arm (run folder `run_val2`); the main arm keeps one validation year. Prediction (Tati): PreVal's advantage over the status quo (H2 contrast) is larger with two validation years, because the stopping and selection signal is less tied to one year. Alternative we will report if it happens: no change or a smaller advantage (the validation block is then on average further from the forecast year). First forecast year of this arm: 2011 (408 and 410 splits).
6. **Horizons (amended 2026-10-19).** Every forecast distance the data allow (1 to 19 years; the bird data end in 2024), as in caribou, analysed in bins 1, 2, 3-4, 5-9, 10+ years. About 970 splits per species before the training rule (about 39,000 models). Long horizons come from early, short windows by construction; the analysis reports the horizon bins separately and the forecast year stays the unit of inference.
7. **Training-share rule (amended 2026-10-19).** Equal training size across regimes already prevents data-size confounding, so the 80% rule is not needed for the validity of a contrast. The retained share of the status-quo training pool (`trainShare`) is stored for every split and the main analysis keeps splits down to the absolute minimum of training cases; a sensitivity restricted to `trainShare >= 0.8` is reported. (Status: pending Tati's confirmation; until then the code default stays 0.8.)
