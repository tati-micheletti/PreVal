# Birds: analysis plan (written 2026-10-17, BEFORE any model is run)

Amendments are dated and labelled. Anything decided after seeing results is exploratory and says so.

## Scope
- **Two species, each its own experiment:** greenfinch (*Chloris chloris*, EURING 16490) and great spotted woodpecker (*Dendrocopos major*, 8760). Species are NOT a variable: each has its own covariate ranking, its own ladder, its own network settings and its own results. Species are only compared in the summary.
- **Response:** `Reviere` (territory count per route and year), 2005-2024, negative-binomial loss. Rows with `Reviere` = -1 are excluded (observer could not give an exact count; no territory count exists).
- **Unit:** route (fixed location, observed in many years). **Final table for modelling:** one row per route and year with the response, route id, coordinates, spatial block, and all covariates.
- **Covariates (no pruning of correlated ones in the main analysis, as in birdMonitor; ranking by permutation importance with highly correlated covariates, |r| > 0.9, permuted together; the effective number of covariates, from a principal-component count at 90% variance, is reported next to the nominal count):** the ones already prepared (1 km land use and cover, elevation, slope, solar radiation, distance to woodland; climate from CHELSA and the 50 km bioclim windows; 200 m summaries inside each route) plus the extra ones from the predictor review (forest and human layers). Nothing already prepared is dropped. Landscape heterogeneity is rebuilt for 2005-2020 from the land-use and land-cover layers. The beech/oak mast index is NOT used (not needed for a regime comparison).

## Design (same as the caribou refit)
- Split = history window s..e and forecast year T > e. Test set = a fixed random half of the routes of year T, identical in all regimes.
- **PreVal:** train s..e-1, validate on year e. **Status quo:** the same route-years re-allocated at random to train and validation. **Route-grouped CV (added 2026-10-17):** same pool, validation = whole routes (all their years) chosen at random, no buffer; it separates route-identity leakage from spatial autocorrelation. **Spatial-block CV:** same pool, validation = whole spatial blocks of routes (blockCV; block size from the residual-autocorrelation range of a baseline model), training excludes the validation blocks and a buffer. **Random CV reference:** random draw from s..T minus the test set (leaky; used only as the H1 reference, never compared with PreVal).
- **Balance (amended 2026-10-17):** equal pool, equal validation size (PreVal's), equal test set, shared model seeds per cell, and EQUAL TRAINING SIZE: every regime is randomly subsampled to the training size of the spatial-block regime (which loses its buffer). Splits where spatial blocking leaves less than 80% of the status-quo training set are skipped. Leak gates stop the run on any violation; a hard unit test checks zero overlap.
- **Network:** one scoring network per species, settings chosen by a pilot on pre-test history only (never on test years), then FIXED and identical for every regime and every ladder level of that species. Output head: negative binomial.
- **Ladder:** covariates ranked by permutation importance of a global model per species; levels 2, 5, 10, 15, 25, 35, 50 and beyond if enough covariates exist.
- **Inference:** the forecast year is the independent unit; exact sign-flip tests across years, t intervals, plus a mixed model on all splits. Shape of the complexity curve: PEAK and END contrasts and the mixed-model regime x count interaction (as in caribou amendment 4), and the per-year stability of each covariate's coefficient (negative-binomial model per year, heterogeneity I-squared and sign flips) as a pre-declared diagnostic.

## Predictions (Tati, 2026-10-17, and the engine's checks)
1. **H1:** the status quo's forecast loss is larger than the random-CV reference's, and the status quo's reported error understates its forecast error (optimism > 0).
2. **H2:** PreVal forecasts better than the status quo.
3. **Spatial-block CV** is not as good as PreVal but better than the status quo: forecast loss PreVal < spatial-block < status quo. Reason for the second half: in a panel the same route appears in many years, so random validation lets a route's other years sit in training; blocks remove that route-level leakage but still validate inside the training years.
4. **Route-grouped CV** (our addition): forecast loss status quo >= route-grouped >= spatial-block >= PreVal (weakly ordered). The gap from the status quo to route-grouped measures route-identity leakage, route-grouped to spatial-block the spatial neighbours, spatial-block to PreVal the forward step in time.
4b. **Optimism** (forecast loss minus the loss the validation reported): largest for the status quo, intermediate for spatial-block CV, smallest for PreVal.
5. **Covariate ladder:** similar to caribou. Under PreVal the loss does not rise with the number of covariates (flat or slightly improving). Under the status quo the loss rises and may form a hump at intermediate counts, driven by covariates whose relationship with the counts changes over time.
6. **Risk noted in advance:** because routes repeat across years, random CV may leak more than in caribou, so the status-quo optimism may be larger than in caribou.

## Sensitivity analyses
- S1: the same experiment with 2 km spatial thinning of the route-years.
- S2: block size x0.5 and x2.
- Optional, only if a reviewer asks (decided 2026-10-17 not to plan it): pruning of correlated covariates (|r| < 0.7, birdMonitor's threshold). The effective number of covariates is reported instead.
- S3 (potential improvement, later): other algorithms on the same splits (birdMonitor's `nnet`, BRT) to see whether the conclusions depend on the network type.

## Rules
Predictions are not changed after seeing results. Differences between this plan and what is run are listed in the results document.

## Amendments of 2026-10-17 (made while building the code, BEFORE any real run; no model result has been looked at)
1. **Network settings by pilot.** For each species a pilot fits a small grid (hidden layers 32-16, 64-32, 128-64, 128-64-32; route embedding 0 or 8; weight decay 0 or 1e-4; dropout 0 or 0.1) on the PRE-TEST history only: train on the earlier years, validate on the last pre-test year (PreVal logic). The smallest network within 0.002 of the best validation loss is kept and then FIXED for every regime, ladder level and forecast year of that species. Pre-test history = years up to the year before the first forecast year of the design.
2. **Covariate ladder.** Primary ranking: permutation importance of one global model with a random 20% hold-out over all years (what a practitioner with the usual random validation would obtain; same as the caribou refit). Sensitivity ladder: the same procedure with a FORWARD hold-out on strictly pre-test years only (the ordering PreVal's own logic gives, never using a forecast year). Correlated covariates (|r| > 0.9) are permuted together and share the group's importance; within a group they are ordered by marginal association with the response. The ordering is identical in all regimes, so it cannot favour a regime by construction.
3. **Design parameters.** Analysis years 2005-2024; history window s..e of at least 5 years; forecast distance 1 year (T = e + 1); every start year s that gives such a window. Spatial blocks: block size = the range of the residual spatial autocorrelation (blockCV::cv_spatial_autocor) of a negative-binomial baseline (top-10 ranked covariates + year factor) fitted to the pre-test rows, route-averaged residuals; buffer = a quarter of the block size; whole blocks (random order) are held out until the PreVal validation size is reached. Splits where blocking leaves less than 80% of the status-quo training pool are skipped and listed (splitsSkipped.csv); they are mostly early, short windows where the PreVal validation year is a large share of the pool.
4. **Land-use hedges.** The land-use product has a hedges class only in 2017-2021 and 2024; in those years hedges are carved out of grassland, orchards and fallow, which would act as a structural break (a covariate whose meaning changes in time). Main analysis: one merged class `green_permanent` = grassland + orchards_and_berries + fallow + hedges (hedges only in the years where they are real) plus a static hedge density per route. Sensitivity (S5): the classes as delivered.
5. **Response.** `Reviere` = -1 (observer could not give an exact number) rows are excluded (4 greenfinch and 5 woodpecker route-years). Primary scores: negative-binomial negative log-likelihood per case (reported on the regime's own validation set, realised on the shared test set); the forecast year is the unit of inference.
6. **Not changed:** all predictions above, the five regimes, equal training size, the pre-specified contrasts (H1 = status quo minus Internal; PreVal is never compared with Internal).

## Amendments of 2026-10-18 (decisions of Tati; still before any real run; they supersede the design parameters of the 2026-10-17 amendments)
1. **Primary ladder = random-holdout permutation importance over all years** (as in caribou). Rationale: every regime gets the same ordering, so the ordering cannot favour a regime; the aim is a regime comparison, not the best model. The forward ordering (strictly pre-test years) stays as a sensitivity ladder.
2. **Design.** Analysis years 2005-2024; PreVal windows with at least 2 TRAINING years; forecast distances 1 to 5 years (as in caribou, where the PreVal advantage grew with the horizon: -0.010 at 1 year, about -0.05 at 3 or more); spatial buffer = block size / 8 (4.5 km for a 36 km block). With these settings the first forecast year that survives the 80% training rule is 2009 (greenfinch 593 splits, 23,720 models; woodpecker 592 splits, 23,680 models), so the network pilot uses years <= 2008 and the design refuses any forecast year <= the last pilot year.
3. **Small Woody Features (Copernicus) are NOT used**: the Thuenen hedge layer is already in (static hedge density per route).
4. **Route information in the network (fixed by design, not chosen by the pilot).** Main analysis: a route INTERCEPT, one learned number per route added to the predicted log-mean and shrunk by an L2 penalty (the network analogue of the random route effect of mixed models and of the route effects of the BBS hierarchical models); the penalty strength is tuned in the pilot on pre-test years. Sensitivity: no route information; and an 8-dimensional route embedding fed with the covariates (as in the caribou network). Units unseen in training use the mean of the trained rows. Prediction: the gain from route information is larger where routes are shared between training and validation (status quo) than where they are not (route-grouped, spatial, PreVal), i.e. it is a leakage channel; we report the status-quo optimism under all three settings.
5. **Two-year validation arm (extra arm, Tati's hypothesis).** PreVal validates on the last TWO years of the window (training >= 2 years before them); the other regimes keep the same validation size (twice as large), the same equal training size and the same shared test set. Run as a separate arm (run folder `run_val2`); the main arm keeps one validation year. Prediction (Tati): PreVal's advantage over the status quo (H2 contrast) is larger with two validation years, because the stopping and selection signal is less tied to one year. Alternative we will report if it happens: no change or a smaller advantage (the validation block is then on average further from the forecast year). First forecast year of this arm: 2011 (408 and 410 splits).
6. **Horizons (amended 2026-10-19).** Every forecast distance the data allow (1 to 19 years; the bird data end in 2024), as in caribou, analysed in bins 1, 2, 3-4, 5-9, 10+ years. About 970 splits per species before the training rule (about 39,000 models). Long horizons come from early, short windows by construction; the analysis reports the horizon bins separately and the forecast year stays the unit of inference.
7. **Training-share rule (amended 2026-10-19).** Equal training size across regimes already prevents data-size confounding, so the 80% rule is not needed for the validity of a contrast. The retained share of the status-quo training pool (`trainShare`) is stored for every split and the main analysis keeps splits down to the absolute minimum of training cases; a sensitivity restricted to `trainShare >= 0.8` is reported. (Status: pending Tati's confirmation; until then the code default stays 0.8.)
8. **Network pilot on all years (amended 2026-10-19, Tati).** The pilot only fixes the network's shape, identically for every regime and covariate level, so it cannot favour a regime; it now uses a random 20% hold-out of all years (like the covariate ranking). The strict pre-test version stays available (`PREVAL_PILOTMODE=forward`). Consequence: forecasting starts in 2008 (one-year validation; 2009 for the two-year arm) and the earliest forecast years are no longer lost. Absolute losses may be marginally optimistic for all regimes alike; relative contrasts are not affected. The forward ranking sensitivity trains on the earlier 60% of the years and holds out the later ones.
9. **Training-share rule replaced by a sanity limit (amended 2026-10-19, Tati).** Splits are skipped only if blocking keeps less than 50% of the status-quo training pool or fewer than 200 training cases; with the 4.5 km buffer this excludes nothing (smallest kept share 0.72 with one validation year, 0.65 with two). The kept share is stored per split and a sensitivity analysis restricted to shares >= 0.8 is reported. Design sizes with horizons 1-19: one validation year 969 splits (38,760 models) per species, forecast years 2008-2024; two validation years 816 splits (32,640 models), forecast years 2009-2024. Analysis ends in 2024 (the bird data end there).
10. **Horizons.** Besides the bins 1, 2, 3-4, 5-9, 10+, every forecast distance is reported on its own (`contrasts_by_horizon_each`).
11. **Pattern found in the greenfinch results, stated as a hypothesis BEFORE the woodpecker results are read (2026-10-20).** The greenfinch pooled contrasts hide opposite effects at short and long horizons (realized loss, mean over covariate levels, forecast year = unit). At 1 year ahead: status quo 1.987 < PreVal 2.038 < spatial-block 2.170 (all differences p < 0.0001, 16-17 of 17 years); at 2 years the same order; at 3-4 years no difference; at 5-9 years: spatial 2.181 < PreVal 2.261 < status quo 2.279; at 10+ years: spatial 2.169 < PreVal 2.293 < status quo 2.301. Spatial and route-grouped losses are almost flat across horizons while the status quo's and PreVal's rise, and the spatial and route-grouped models stop training much earlier (median best epoch 6-7 against 37-52). Interpretation (a hypothesis, not a result): route-level memory helps short forecasts and hurts long ones. This was found AFTER looking at the greenfinch results, so it is exploratory for the greenfinch. **Hypothesis for the woodpecker (independent replication):** the same ordering at 1-2 years (status quo < PreVal < spatial), no clear order at 3-4 years, and the reverse at 5+ years (spatial < PreVal <= status quo). The pooled P3 and P4 contrasts of the plan stay reported as pre-specified; the by-horizon contrasts are reported next to them.
12. **Refit arm (POST-HOC, added 2026-10-20 after seeing the greenfinch results; motivated by PreVal's loss at 1-2 years ahead).** In the main design a model is trained on the training cases only and the validation cases decide the stopping epoch. In practice people refit after that choice. Refit regimes: `FutureUnseenRefit` (PreVal) and `FutureTaintedRefit` (status quo): retrain on training + validation cases for the base model's best epoch, with the base model's learning-rate trajectory; nothing is tuned again. No leakage: the validation cases come from years up to e and the forecast year is later; the test cases are never used. The refit models see the validation cases in addition (nTrain = training + validation). Run on the same splits and seeds as the main run; contrasts: PreVal-refit vs status-quo-refit, PreVal-refit vs the plain status quo, and the effect of refitting in each regime (`compareRefit`). Because this arm was designed after seeing the results, it is reported as post-hoc.
13. **Further planned experiments (2026-10-20, not yet run):** (a) spatial-test arm: the test cases of every regime lie in held-out spatial blocks (as in the caribou spatial arm), with a sixth regime that combines temporal and spatial blocking (PreVal + spatial blocks); (b) route information none and 8-number embedding (sensitivities); (c) the two-year-validation arm; (d) a synthetic dataset with known truth (persistent route effects that decay over time plus a covariate effect that drifts) to test whether the horizon-dependent ordering of validation schemes arises from route memory; (e) woodpecker replication of amendment 11 (running); (f) other algorithms (BRT or nnet) as a sensitivity.
14. **Route-grouped CV dropped (Tati, 2026-10-21).** Its results were almost identical to the spatial-block regime's (the only significant difference between them in the greenfinch is 0.007 in loss), so from now on only the spatial-block regime is analysed: the analysis, the figures and every new arm leave route-grouped out (`excludeRegimes`; new designs no longer build it). The main greenfinch and woodpecker runs were trained with it and its models stay on disk; it is not reported. Predictions P4 (the weak ordering that involved route-grouped) and P4b are therefore reported only for the regimes that remain.
15. **Covariate set of the first runs (recorded 2026-10-21).** The first greenfinch and woodpecker runs use the 95 covariates that existed at the time (1 km land use and cover, 200 m summaries, elevation, slope, distance to woodland, CHELSA climate features, 50 km bioclim). The extra layers listed in `PreVal_birds_download_todo` (Copernicus TCD, DLT, FTY, IMD; MODIS NDVI/EVI and tree cover; open data) have NOT been added yet; they are planned.
16. **Training length and comparisons (Tati, 2026-10-21/22).** (a) Comparisons are made ONLY between validation protocols at the same covariate level; the 2-covariate model is no longer used as a reference level in figures or tables (absolute loss and protocol-versus-protocol differences only). (b) All new arms use at least 5 training years (`minTrainYears = 5`): the greenfinch shows that PreVal needs enough training data (median 4,400 training cases; caribou has 50,568); in the main arm, restricted to splits with at least 5 training years (560 splits, forecast years 2011-2024), PreVal minus status quo is +0.042 [0.027, 0.057] at 1-2 years ahead, -0.012 [-0.032, 0.007] at 3-4 years and -0.063 [-0.081, -0.044] at 5+ years (10 forecast years, exact p = 0.002). This subset was chosen after seeing the all-splits results; both are reported. (c) A direct test of data amount versus number of years is planned: cap the training cases of long windows at the size of short windows.
17. **Extra layers, how they are obtained (2026-10-22).** Copernicus TCD, Broadleaved and Coniferous Cover Density at 100 m, 2018-2024, by the Copernicus Data Space API (`birds/download_cdse_clms.R`, 1,449 small files, 938 MB). TCD 2012 and 2015 and the imperviousness layers are not in that catalogue: tree cover for 2005-2017 comes from MODIS MOD44B, built-up surface from GHS-BUILT-S (open). MODIS NDVI/EVI and tree cover from NASA Earthdata.

26. **Priority (Tati, 2026-10-22):** the goal is more robust models, i.e. how a model generalises to out-of-distribution data. The new-places arm (test cases in spatial blocks that no regime ever trains or validates on, and in a LATER year than every training year) is therefore the primary test of that goal; the main arm (same routes, later years) is the secondary, monitoring-scenario test. Both are reported in full. Spatial-and-temporal novelty is built into the new-places design; novelty in covariate space (extreme conditions) is not controlled by it and would be assessed afterwards, per test case, from the distance of its covariates to the training cases (not part of the pre-specified analysis; to be added to the plan before it is run).

## Amendment of 2026-10-23 (Tati: "write it into the plan before anything runs"; no corrected model has been run)
27. **Out-of-distribution analysis by covariate novelty (pre-specified, secondary).** It asks whether a validation protocol protects the model when a test case is unlike the cases it was trained on, in covariate space (extreme years or conditions), which the spatial and temporal separation of the new-places arm does not control.
    a. **Cases and levels.** Every test case of the models at covariate levels 10 and 50, in the new-places arm (primary) and the same-places arm (secondary). Per-case losses are the calibrated ones (`lossCal` in the saved per-case files).
    b. **Novelty measure.** Dissimilarity index DI after Meyer and Pebesma (2021, Methods in Ecology and Evolution; area of applicability). Reference set = PreVal's training pool of the window (years s..e-v, identical for every regime of the window and used for the ranking, so a case has ONE novelty value that does not depend on the regime). Covariates = the first k of the window's ranking (k = 10 or 50), standardised with the mean and SD of the reference set and weighted by the permutation importance in that window's ranking file. DI = Euclidean distance from the test case to its nearest reference case, divided by the mean nearest-neighbour distance among reference cases (leave-one-out, on a random sample of 2,000 reference cases, seeded by the split id).
    c. **Statistics.** (i) Per forecast year and regime, the mean calibrated loss of the test cases in the three DI terciles (terciles of DI over all test cases of the arm and level), and the regime contrasts within each tercile (PreVal minus status quo, spatial minus status quo, PreVal + spatial minus PreVal, PreVal + spatial minus spatial). (ii) Per forecast year, the slope of the per-case loss difference between two regimes on log(DI). Across years: mean, t interval, number of years with each sign, exact sign-flip p-value (the forecast year is the unit, as everywhere else). No threshold is used to call a case "out of distribution"; DI is analysed as a continuous variable and by terciles.
    d. **Primary statement (new-places arm, level 50):** the slope of (PreVal minus status quo) on log(DI), and of (PreVal + spatial minus PreVal). **Prediction, stated before the run:** if validating on the future protects against overfitting to the training distribution, the slope of PreVal minus status quo is negative (PreVal's loss rises less than the status quo's as cases become more novel). No prediction is made for the same-places arm, and none for PreVal + spatial against PreVal.
    e. **Caveats written in advance:** DI measures novelty in the covariates the ranking selected, not in the unmeasured drivers; static route covariates make DI small for routes seen before (so novelty and route memory are partly confounded in the same-places arm, which is why the new-places arm is the primary one); terciles of a skewed measure are coarse.
28. **Names of the two main designs (to avoid confusion):** the "same-places" arm (folder `run`, historically called "main": test cases are half of the routes of the forecast year, which the models may have seen in earlier years) and the "new-places" arm (folder `run_spatialTest`: test cases lie in spatial blocks that no regime trains or validates on). The new-places arm is the primary test (amendment 26); in reports the two are called by these names, not by the folder names.

## Amendment of 2026-10-27 (Tati: "why isn't PreVal ALSO on unseen routes?"; written before any model of the new regime was run; the other arms had been read)
31. **New regime FutureUnseenGrouped = "PreVal on unseen routes" (PreValRoutes).** PreVal's validation year contains routes that the model also saw in earlier years, so it can reward route memory (route intercept, fingerprint covariates). PreValRoutes keeps PreVal's temporal logic (training = earlier years, validation = the LAST year) but takes as validation the last-year cases of WHOLE ROUTES that are absent from the training set in EVERY year. Rules: (a) validation size = the size of PreVal + spatial's validation in the same split (shared block draw, seed+20; fallback max(10, 15% of the normal validation size)); (b) the routes are drawn with seed+50 from the routes present in the last year; (c) training = the common training size (equal to all other regimes, subsampled from the earlier years without those routes); (d) NO spatial buffer (this is the difference from PreVal + spatial: it removes route memory but not spatial autocorrelation between neighbouring routes); (e) test cases, seeds, splits and everything else are those of the existing arms; the regime is trained in separate run folders `run_routes` (same places) and `run_routesNew` (new places) with the same plan, manifests and per-window rankings (105 of 105 rankings reused; gates L3/L4/L7 and T4/T7 pass in all 560 splits per arm; training and test sizes equal to the existing arms in all splits). Implemented in `regimeSplits.R`, `spatialTestSplits.R`, `generatePlan.R` (`routeHeldOut`), tested in `test_splits.R`, `test_spatialtest.R`.
    **Predictions, stated before the run:** (i) new places: PreValRoutes minus PreVal < 0 (removing route memory from the validation set makes the stopping point and tuning more honest for unseen places); (ii) new places: PreValRoutes minus PreVal + spatial >= 0 (without the spatial buffer, validation routes still neighbour training routes), and PreValRoutes minus status quo < 0; (iii) same places: PreValRoutes minus PreVal is positive at horizon 1 (the one-year forecast of seen routes profits from route memory) and not positive at long horizons; (iv) the best epoch of PreValRoutes is shorter than that of PreVal. If (i) or (ii) fails, the explanation "validation containing seen routes" is not supported for the new-places gap between PreVal and the spatial regimes. Compared with `compareAddedRegime` (`birds/post_analysis_birds.R`, part `routes`): PreValRoutes minus PreVal, status quo, spatial and PreVal + spatial, overall, by horizon and by number of covariates; the forecast year is the unit; plus its optimism (realized minus reported).
32. **Covariate-transferability hypothesis (exploratory, to be written fully when the species are chosen; no model has been run).** Observation (from `covariate_transferability_<species>.csv`, associations only): in greenfinch and woodpecker the strong covariates do not change sign across years, only 3-4% of covariates are unstable, so the status quo has little to overfit; this fits the observed small PreVal advantage in the same places. Hypothesis: when the MAIN covariates of a species flip or drift (weather-anomaly covariates are the natural candidates and matter for climate-change forecasting), (a) the status quo validation (random split of the same years) selects covariates and stops later than PreVal (longer best epoch), (b) its forecast loss rises faster with the number of covariates than that of PreVal, and (c) PreVal's gain over the status quo is larger than in non-flipping species. Species are chosen by a screen of associations with counts only (89 species; no model result is used): the four candidates are species with at least 4,600 records and 13-16 flipping covariates; greenfinch and woodpecker serve as the non-flipping contrast (supplement). No covariate ordering is manipulated; the ladder is the natural ranking extended to levels 100 and 157. All regimes, the new-places arm and the novelty analysis are run exactly as for the first two species. The statement is only made if (c) holds in the new-places arm over the 14 forecast years with the year as unit.

## Amendment of 2026-10-29 (Tati: "Could we do birds like this? ... people can't say any results are because of different data"; written before any model of this arm was run)
33. **Full-pool arm ("caribou-style"), same-places design, both species; folder `run_fullPool`.** Why: in the main design every regime's training set is randomly cut to the SMALLEST training pool among all regimes built together (the spatial regime loses its buffer), with a different random cut per regime, so PreVal and the status quo train on the same pool but not on the same cases, and about 20% of the cases are lost (median training size 5,800 instead of 7,229). The old caribou design had no cut: PreVal and the status quo used exactly the same strata. The full-pool arm builds ONLY PreVal, status quo and Internal (`PREVAL_REGIMES=FutureUnseen,FutureTainted,Internal`), so the common training size is the whole pool and nothing is cut; Internal draws only from routes that appear in the pool of years s..e (`PREVAL_MATCHUNITS=1`, the birds version of the old caribou `matchAnimals`). Same 560 splits, same test cases, seeds and per-window rankings as the main arm; 11,760 models per species; all gates pass, including the matched-Internal check (`matchUnits` in `buildRegimeSplits`, tested in `test_splits.R`). No new-places version exists because the spatial regimes need the cut to define the test blocks.
    **Predictions, stated before the run:** (i) pooled PreVal minus status quo in this arm is within +-0.02 of its value in the main arm (about 0 in the same places); a value of -0.02 or lower in at least 11 of 14 years would mean that the random cut explained part of the difference between the birds and caribou; (ii) H1 (status-quo optimism minus Internal's) stays positive and its size is reported for the matched Internal next to the unmatched one of the main arm; (iii) all other contrasts of the main arm that involve only these three regimes keep their sign. Compared with `compareRefit`-style paired contrasts on the same splits (post-analysis part `arms`, to be extended).
