# Caribou transferability screen: reading (run of 2026-10-29, descriptive, before any engine run)

In caribou, the covariates that change their effect from year to year are almost all movement terms: step length (`logSl`), turn angle (`cosTa`, `sinTa`) and the nine interactions of step length with habitat, which together make up the top of the first-pass importance order. Of the 21 covariates that a conditional logit can identify (the nine `*_start` main effects are constant within a stratum and cancel out), 10 have at least 3 of the 10 years with the opposite sign, and 9 of those are movement terms; the tenth is the share of needleleaf forest at the end of the step. The flip share among the identifiable covariates in the top 2, 5, 10, 20 and 30 is 1 of 2, 4 of 5, 7 of 10, 10 of 20 and 10 of 21 (the first-pass ranking, which is not the engine's per-window ranking). Most end-of-step habitat effects keep their sign (wetland and distance to paved roads are positive in all 10 years, mixed forest negative in all 10), although some change in size (timeSinceHarvest and timeSinceFire have I² of 0.99 and 0.90). Because the interactions are tied to `logSl` by construction, this is one flipping group and not ten; counting families, there are two independent flipping groups (movement, and needleleaf cover at one covariate), and one if only strict flips (a significantly positive and a significantly negative year) are counted. Two checks say that this should not be read as ecology yet. First, the yearly `logSl` effect follows the mean observed log step length almost perfectly (Spearman 0.99 over the 10 years) and, more loosely, the fix interval (0.5): the collar schedule changes over time (the share of 8-h fixes falls from 97% in 2018-19 to 61% in 2022, and 4-h and other intervals grow), and when only strata with the commonest 8-h interval are kept, sign flips fall from 10 covariates to 4 (top-k shares 0, 0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.19), although the yearly effects still differ in size (I² of `logSl` stays 0.93, and 9 covariates still have both a significantly positive and a significantly negative year). Second, the animals change (26 animals in 2013, 64 in 2020; 9 to 39% of each year's animals are new), but keeping only the 83 animals seen in at least 3 years leaves the picture unchanged (11 flipping covariates, 3 groups), so the change in animals alone does not explain it. The reading for the plan: caribou has a clear flipping group at the top of its ladder, but that group is the movement block, and its flips partly reflect how the available steps and the fix schedule change over time, so P5 (PreVal gains more where the top covariates are unstable) cannot separate "covariates that do not transfer" from "movement-model mismatch and schedule changes". Limits: the ranking is the first-pass one (all years); the joint models failed to converge in 2015 and 2022 (all variants) and are not used; with 10 years, I² and the sign counts are descriptive only, not tests.
